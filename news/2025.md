## 2025.12.31

### Goodwill Does Not Circulate Without Conditions
#### — An Interim Report Based on Field Verification —

Through the on-the-ground operation of the Contribution Protocol (CP), this article reports interim findings derived from practical observation.

We found that goodwill does not circulate easily unless it is accompanied by

gratitude from recipients,
proactive actions that lead to the next act of support, and
financially fair and equitable compensation for providers.

When goodwill lacks clearly defined conditions of reciprocity, it gradually becomes treated as an “entitled service.”
As expectations increase, demands accumulate, and the financial and psychological burden ultimately concentrates on providers.
In such cases, goodwill does not function as a sustainable system, but instead results in the personal exhaustion of individuals.

This report is based on a PDCA cycle that involved real-world verification of multiple funding models, including government subsidies and quasi-private grants.

As a result of examining institutional sustainability, it was confirmed that incorporating conditional funds (such as subsidies or uncompensated goodwill) into ongoing system operations poses significant design risks.
This report does not assess the scale of subsidies or the merits of specific legal or organizational forms; rather, it focuses on funding design for long-term institutional operation.

We also examined donations as a potential funding source.
However, they did not function as a sustainable stream of funding and remained limited to one-time contributions.

From this outcome, we concluded that donations are unsuitable as a permanent funding base for system operation, and that institutional design should not depend on them.

This decision does not represent a retreat.
It is a corrective adjustment aimed at ensuring that goodwill is not consumed as personal sacrifice, but instead sustained through institutional structures.

Going forward, we will continue system design and verification without assuming ongoing personal goodwill or individual burden.
Specifically, we will prioritize structures in which responsibilities and conditions of reciprocity are explicitly embedded within the system itself, rather than relying on personal judgment or sacrifice.

We will continue to organize and share only those conditions that are reproducible at the institutional level, based on insights gained through field practice.


## 2025.12.6

### Publication of Real Operational Data from the Contribution Protocol.

The Contribution Protocol (CP) is a small contribution-based currency system currently being piloted within our local support activities.
Our latest data analysis shows that the reserve ratio at issuance and the recovery ratio at redemption both recorded the same value: 67%.

• Reserve Ratio
The portion set aside in advance to prepare for future use.
• Recovery Ratio
The portion that actually returns to the system when the currency is used.

This is the first time these two ratios have aligned, making it an interesting trend that emerged naturally during day-to-day operations.
While the result is neither “good” nor “bad” in itself, the fact that the reserve and recovery ratios matched indicates that, during this period, issuance and usage were occurring without major imbalance or disruption.
CP is still a small-scale operation, but being able to observe and verify the system’s behavior through real data is highly encouraging.
